For my mother, always









1
People come from everywhere to forget.
I set down the pitcher of water I’m holding and step off the porch. A cloud of dust kicks up behind a rust-colored station wagon making its way up our drive. It slides next to the other cars lined up on our front yard. The large woman hunched behind the steering wheel looks no different from the rest of the folks who make the pilgrimage here: tired from a long drive, heavy with unwanted memories, and badly in need of a pee.
I squint against the glare of the sun to get a better look at the license plate. Vivi told me and Mama not to hold our breath—Hawaii and Alaska are too far, even for the most memory-burdened people.
